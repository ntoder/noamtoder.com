Dots and Boxes

Take turns drawing a line between two dots. If your line closes a box, you win that box and get another turn!
Whoever owns the most boxes at the end wins.

Game Over

Never draw the third side of a box unless you have to: the computer will close it and keep going.

About Dots and Boxes

The classic pencil-and-paper game. You and the computer take turns joining two neighbouring dots with a line. Draw the fourth side of a box and you claim it and play again. Early on it's a game of patience, avoiding giving away boxes. At the end, long chains of boxes open up, and the player who forces the other to open them usually wins.

How to play

Click or tap between two dotsDraw a line

Tips

  • Don't draw the third side of any box.
  • Count the chains near the end: make the computer open the long ones.
  • Sometimes giving away two boxes lets you keep control of a long chain.

How to get better at Dots and Boxes

In the beginning, the safe move is any line that doesn't make a box with three sides. As long as both players play safely, nobody scores. Sooner or later, though, every remaining move gives something away, and that's when the real game starts.

By then the board has turned into chains, rows of boxes that are connected. When one box in a chain is opened, the other player can take the whole chain. So when you're forced to give something away, give away the shortest chain.

Expert players use a trick: when taking a long chain, they leave the last two boxes for the opponent by drawing the line that splits them. The opponent takes two boxes but then has to open the next chain. Try it against the computer!

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Win by never drawing the third side of a box.
  • Getting good: Count chains and always give away the shortest one.
  • Expert: Use the 'leave two boxes' trick to stay in control.

Did you know?

Dots and Boxes was first described in 1889 by the French mathematician Édouard Lucas, who also invented the Tower of Hanoi puzzle.

Questions players ask

Why do I get another turn?

Closing a box always gives you an extra move. You can chain several boxes in one turn.

How big is the board?

Five by five boxes, so 25 boxes in total.
