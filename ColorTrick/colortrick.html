Color Trick

Read what the TOP word MEANS. Then look at the INK COLOUR of the BOTTOM word.
Do they match? Answer YES (➡) or NO (⬅) as fast as you can. You have 60 seconds, and a wrong answer costs 3 seconds!

Time's up!

Ignore what the bottom word says. Only its colour matters. Squinting at it can help!

About Color Trick

A brain teaser based on a famous psychology experiment. Two coloured words appear. The question is whether the meaning of the top word matches the ink colour of the bottom word. It sounds easy, but your brain reads words automatically, and that gets in the way when the bottom word says one colour but is printed in another. Answer as many as you can in 60 seconds. More colours appear as your score goes up.

How to play

➡ or J or YYes, they match
⬅ or F or NNo, they don't

Tips

  • The colour of the top word doesn't matter at all.
  • What the bottom word says doesn't matter either.
  • Every 5 right in a row gives bonus points.

How to get better at Color Trick

Color Trick only cares about two things: what the top word means and what colour the bottom word is printed in. The top word's ink and the bottom word's text are distractions. Train yourself to read the top word and only look at the colour of the bottom one.

A useful trick is to say the top word in your head, then glance at the bottom word without reading it, as if it were just a blob of colour. Some players unfocus their eyes slightly on the bottom word.

Speed matters, but wrong answers cost 3 seconds, which is more than you'd lose by thinking for a moment. Find a steady rhythm. As your score rises, more colours join, so the game gets harder just as you get faster.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Score 15.
  • Getting good: Score 30 with no mistakes.
  • Expert: Score 50.

Did you know?

This puzzle is based on the Stroop effect, discovered by psychologist John Ridley Stroop in 1935. Naming the ink colour of a word that spells a different colour takes most people noticeably longer.

Questions players ask

Which word's colour do I look at?

Only the bottom word's ink colour. For the top word, only its meaning counts.

Why is it so hard?

Reading is automatic for your brain, so the word's meaning gets in the way of noticing its colour.
