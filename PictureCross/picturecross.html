Picture Cross

The numbers next to each row and above each column tell you how many squares in a row to paint, in order. For example, “3 1” means a group of 3, a gap, then 1.
Paint the right squares to reveal the hidden picture! Switch to ✖ to mark squares you know are empty.

About Picture Cross

A picture logic puzzle, also called a nonogram. Every row and column has number clues saying how many squares are painted and in what groups. Use logic to work out which squares to fill, and a little pixel picture appears: a heart, a cat, a rocket and more. Mistakes are shown straight away, so it's friendly for beginners.

How to play

Click or tap (drag for many)Paint or cross out squares
Right-click or XCross out / switch mode

Tips

  • Rows with a 0 clue are completely empty.
  • Big clues overlap in the middle: those squares must be painted.
  • Cross out squares you know are empty to keep track.

How to get better at Picture Cross

Start with the easy lines. A clue of 0 means the whole line is empty, and a clue equal to the line's length means it's completely full. Mark those first.

Next, use overlap. If a line is 8 long and the clue is 6, the group of 6 must cover the middle 4 squares, no matter where it starts. The same trick works with several groups: add them up with one gap between each, and see how much room is left over.

Crossing out empty squares is just as important as painting. Every square you cross out makes the other clues easier to solve, and finished lines are crossed out for you automatically.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Finish the 8 by 8 pictures without mistakes.
  • Getting good: Solve the first 10 by 10 picture.
  • Expert: Complete all twelve pictures.

Did you know?

Picture logic puzzles were invented in Japan in the late 1980s and became popular around the world in newspapers, just like crosswords and sudoku.

Questions players ask

What happens if I make a mistake?

The square shows as a cross and counts as a mistake. Fewer mistakes give a bigger bonus.

Can I drag to paint?

Yes, hold and drag across squares to paint or cross out several at once.
