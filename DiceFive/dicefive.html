About Dice Five

A classic dice game for one player. Roll five dice up to three times a turn, holding the ones you want to keep. Then choose a box on the score card: the top half scores ones through sixes, the bottom half scores poker-style combinations like a full house, straights and five of a kind. Each box can only be filled once, so sometimes you have to take a zero. Plan well to get the highest total.

How to get better at Dice Five

Dice Five is about choosing what to keep. After your first roll, look for the strongest start: a pair or three of a kind points toward the top section and the 'of a kind' boxes, while 2-3-4 points toward the straights. Hold those dice and reroll the rest.

The top section has a hidden prize: if your ones-through-sixes add up to 63 or more, you get 35 bonus points. That's three of each number. Getting four 5s or 6s early gives you room to be short somewhere else.

Every box must be filled, so sometimes you have to put a zero somewhere. When a turn goes badly, put the zero in a box that's hard to get anyway, like Dice Five or the 1s. Save Chance for an emergency.

Did you know?

The chance of rolling five of the same number in a single roll of five dice is 6 in 7,776, or about 1 in 1,300.

Questions players ask

What's a full house?

Three of one number and two of another, like 4-4-4-2-2. It's worth 25.

What's the difference between the straights?

A small straight is 4 numbers in a row (30 points), a large straight is 5 in a row (40 points).
