About Sand Box

A falling-sand toy where every tiny grain follows simple rules. Sand falls and piles into hills. Water flows and spreads out. Stone and wood stay where you put them. Plants grow by drinking nearby water. Fire burns wood and plants and turns into smoke, and lava flows slowly, sets things alight and hardens into stone when it touches water. Mix them together and see what happens.

How to get better at Sand Box

Sand Box has no goal. It's a little science lab. Every grain follows a simple rule: sand falls and slides off piles, water falls and spreads sideways, steam and smoke rise. Lots of simple rules together make surprisingly realistic results.

Try experiments. Build a stone container and fill it with water. Plant seeds next to the water and watch them grow. Then drop fire on the plants and see how fast it spreads.

Lava is the most powerful material. It flows slowly, sets wood and plants on fire, and when it touches water it hardens into stone and makes steam. You can build volcano islands by pouring lava into a lake.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Grow a plant using water.
  • Getting good: Turn lava into stone.
  • Expert: Build a castle that survives a fire.

Did you know?

Falling-sand games are a simple kind of cellular automaton: a grid where every cell changes based on its neighbours. A famous example is the Game of Life, invented in 1970.
