About Block Fit

A relaxing block puzzle on an 8 by 8 board. You get three blocks at a time: drag each one onto the board wherever it fits. Fill a whole row or column and it clears, giving you room again. Clearing two or more lines at once, or clearing on turn after turn, builds a combo that multiplies your points. There's no timer and no falling pieces. The game only ends when none of your three blocks can fit.

Tips

  • Fill the edges and corners first, and keep the middle open.
  • Always leave room for a 3x3 square and a long 1x5 line.
  • Clearing a row and a column at the same time scores much more than one line.
  • Keep clearing on every turn or two to keep your streak going.
  • Blocks that turn grey don't fit anywhere right now.

How to get better at Block Fit

Block Fit is all about space. Every turn you get three blocks, and you must place all three before you get new ones, so before you drop the first one, check that the other two will still fit. The biggest troublemakers are the 3x3 square and the long 1x5 lines, so try to always keep one empty 3x3 area and one clear row or column.

Build along the edges and corners. Blocks in the middle split the board into small pockets that only single squares can fill. If you stack against the walls, your empty space stays in big, useful pieces.

Points come mostly from combos. Clearing two lines with one block scores four times as much as one line, and clearing again within the next few moves keeps your 🔥 streak growing. When the board is nearly full, aim for the shot that clears a line even if it isn't pretty: every clear buys you more room.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Just learn to clear lines. Place every block so it finishes a row or column if you can.
  • Getting good: Start planning for all three blocks before you place the first one.
  • Expert: Set up two lines that one block can finish at the same time, and keep your streak alive.

Did you know?

Puzzles about packing shapes together are much older than video games. The word 'polyomino', for shapes made of joined squares, was introduced by the mathematician Solomon Golomb in 1953.

Questions players ask

When does the game end?

When none of your remaining blocks can fit anywhere on the board. There's no timer.

Can I rotate the blocks?

No, each block comes in a fixed direction. Part of the challenge is planning around the shapes you get.

How do combos work?

Clearing more lines at once multiplies the points, and clearing again within your next few moves builds a streak that multiplies them even more.

What do grey blocks mean?

A grey block doesn't fit anywhere right now. Clear a line to make room for it.
