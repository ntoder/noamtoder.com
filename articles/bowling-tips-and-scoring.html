Bowling tips and how bowling scoring really works

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

Ten-pin bowling looks simple: roll the ball, knock down pins. But the scoring confuses lots of people, and where you aim matters more than how hard you throw. Here's how both work.

The basics

A game has 10 frames. In each frame you get up to two rolls to knock down all 10 pins. Each pin you knock down is worth a point, but strikes and spares earn bonuses.

Strikes and spares

  • Strike (X): all 10 pins with your first roll. You score 10 plus the pins from your next two rolls.
  • Spare (/): all 10 pins using both rolls. You score 10 plus the pins from your next roll.
  • Open frame: pins left standing after two rolls. You just score the pins you knocked down.

That's why a strike's score sometimes isn't shown straight away: the game is waiting for your next two rolls to work it out.

An example

Frame 1: strike. Frame 2: 7 then 2. The strike is worth 10 + 7 + 2 = 19. Frame 2 is worth 9. Total after two frames: 28.

The tenth frame

If you get a strike or spare in the last frame, you get extra rolls so the bonus can be counted: two more rolls after a strike, one more after a spare.

The perfect game

Twelve strikes in a row (ten frames plus the two bonus rolls in the tenth) scores the maximum of 300. Each frame is worth 30: 10 for the strike plus 10 and 10 for the next two strikes.

Where to aim

The best place to hit isn't the front pin straight on. Hitting the head pin dead centre often leaves pins standing on both sides. Instead, aim for the pocket: the gap between the front pin and one of the two pins behind it (for a right-handed bowler, between pins 1 and 3). Hitting the pocket at a slight angle makes the pins knock each other over like dominoes, and strikes become much more likely.

That's also why good bowlers roll a curving hook ball: coming in at an angle spreads the pins better than a straight ball.

Spares win games

Strikes are exciting, but steady spares matter more for most players. A game of all spares (with average bonus rolls) still scores well over 150. Practise aiming your second roll straight at the leftover pins rather than going for power.

Play

Bowling on the Hub uses real 10-frame scoring, so you can watch the strike and spare bonuses add up. Try aiming for the pocket instead of the middle and see what happens to your score.
