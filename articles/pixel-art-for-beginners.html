Pixel art for beginners: make your first sprite

By Noam · 15 November 2026 · 6 min read

Pixel art is drawing one square at a time. It comes from early video games, when computers could only show small images with a few colours, and artists had to place every pixel by hand. Today it's popular because it looks clean and charming, and because anyone can start: there are no brush skills to learn, just squares.

Here's how to make your first small picture, called a sprite, in Pixel Paint.

1. Start small

Pick the 16 by 16 grid. It feels tiny, but that's the point: with only 256 squares, every pixel matters and you learn to keep things simple. Many classic game characters were this size or smaller.

2. Choose a simple subject

Good first sprites are things with a clear shape: a heart, a mushroom, a sword, a slime, a cat's face or a little spaceship. Avoid detailed subjects like a whole person in a scene. You can try those later on a bigger grid.

3. Draw the outline in a dark colour

Use a dark colour, not necessarily black. Dark blue or dark brown often look softer. Draw the outer shape first. Keep lines one pixel thick, and when drawing curves, use steps that change gradually, like 3 pixels, then 2, then 1. Uneven steps make curves look bumpy.

4. Use mirror mode for symmetry

Lots of sprites are symmetrical: faces, hearts, spaceships, potions. Switch on mirror mode, and everything you draw on one side appears on the other. It halves the work and makes the result look tidy.

5. Fill with a few colours

Use the fill bucket to colour big areas. Limit yourself to four or five colours for the whole sprite. A small palette makes pixel art look deliberate and clean, and it's much easier to make the colours work together.

6. Add simple shading

Decide where the light comes from, say the top left. Then pick a lighter shade of each colour for the side facing the light, and a darker shade for the side facing away. Even one or two pixels of highlight make a sprite look round instead of flat. A small white pixel in an eye makes a character look alive.

7. Step back and fix

Pixel art can look messy up close and great at its real size. Lean back from the screen every so often. If a pixel looks out of place, change it. Use undo freely: Ctrl+Z takes back your last step.

8. Save and share

Pixel Paint saves your picture automatically in your browser, so you can come back to it later. When you're happy, press Save picture to download it as an image.

Ideas for your next sprite

  • Draw the same character with a happy face and a sad face.
  • Make a set of three items for a game: a coin, a key and a heart.
  • Try a 32 by 32 grid and draw a small landscape with a sky, hills and a sun.
