Solitaire strategy: how to win more games of Klondike

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

Klondike is the solitaire game most people know: seven columns of cards, a stock pile, and four foundation piles you build up from Ace to King. Some games can't be won no matter what you do, but many games that people lose could have been won with better choices. These tips will help you win more often.

1. Uncover hidden cards first

The face-down cards in the columns are the biggest problem in Klondike, because you can't plan around cards you can't see. When you have a choice of moves, prefer the one that turns over a hidden card. Moves from the tableau (the seven columns) are usually better than moves from the stock pile for this reason.

2. Work on the biggest columns

The column on the right starts with six hidden cards. Freeing those cards opens up the game more than anything else. When two moves seem equally good, pick the one that helps the column with the most face-down cards.

3. Only empty a column if you have a King to put there

Only a King can go into an empty column. An empty space with no King ready is wasted. Before clearing a column, check that there's a King (ideally one with useful cards under it) that can move in.

4. Choose your King carefully

If you have a choice of Kings for an empty column, think about colours. If the black Queen you need is still buried, a red King is more useful, because the cards you'll want to build on it are the ones you can reach.

5. Don't rush cards to the foundations

Aces and Twos should go up straight away. But moving higher cards to the foundations too early can leave you stuck, because those cards might be needed in the tableau to hold lower cards of the opposite colour. A good rule: move a card up only when it isn't needed to build on, or when both colours below it are already up.

6. Play the stock pile thoughtfully

When you draw from the stock, think about which cards you'll see on the next pass. In draw-three mode, playing one card from the waste changes which cards come up next time, which can be used on purpose to reach a card you need.

7. Keep your options open

Sometimes the best move is to wait. If a move doesn't uncover anything or create space, and there's nothing else to gain from it, it's often better to draw a card and see what comes up.

Draw one or draw three?

Draw-one is easier, because you see every card in the stock on every pass. Draw-three is harder and needs more planning, since only every third card is available. If you're learning, start with draw-one and switch to draw-three when you're winning often.

A bit of history

Patience games, as solitaire is called in Britain, have been played since at least the late 1700s. Klondike became world-famous when it was included with Windows 3.0 in 1990, partly to help people practise using a mouse by dragging cards.

Play

Solitaire on the Hub lets you choose draw one or draw three. Try the "uncover hidden cards first" rule for a few games and see how your win rate changes.
