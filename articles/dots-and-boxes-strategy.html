Dots and Boxes: the chain trick that wins almost every game

By Noam · 25 November 2026 · 6 min read

Dots and Boxes is the paper-and-pencil game where players take turns drawing a line between two dots. Close the fourth side of a box and you claim it and draw again. It was described by the French mathematician Édouard Lucas (the same person who invented the Tower of Hanoi) in 1889. Most people play it by grabbing every box they can, but strong players know a trick that wins game after game.

The beginning: don't give anything away

Early on, the rule is simple: never draw the third side of a box, because your opponent will just take it. Players keep drawing "safe" lines until there are none left. At that point the board is full of chains: corridors of boxes where drawing one line lets the other player take the whole corridor.

Chains decide the game

When the safe lines run out, someone has to open a chain. Whoever opens it gives the boxes away. So the real game is about who will be forced to open the long chains at the end. Short chains (one or two boxes) don't matter much. Long chains (three or more) are where the points are.

The double-cross

Here's the trick. When your opponent opens a long chain for you, take all the boxes except the last two. Instead of closing those two, draw the line that leaves them as a pair for your opponent. They get two boxes, but now they have to move again, and the only moves left open the next chain, which you take. Giving away two boxes to win the next chain is usually a great deal. This move is called a double-cross.

The long-chain rule

There's a counting rule that tells you, early in the game, whether you're on track to be the one in control at the end. On a board with an even number of dots, the first player wants an odd number of long chains; the second player wants an even number. You don't need to memorise it to get better. Just start noticing chains as they form, and try to make or break them so you're not the one forced to open the last long one.

Simple habits that help

  • Never draw the third side of a box unless you have no choice.
  • Late in the game, if you must give something away, give the smallest chain.
  • When you're handed a long chain, remember the double-cross: leave the last two boxes.

Play it

Dots and Boxes on the Hub lets you play the computer or a friend. Try the double-cross the next time the computer opens a long chain for you, and watch the score swing.
