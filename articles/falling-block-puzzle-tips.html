Falling-block puzzle tips: keep your stack flat and clear more lines

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

Falling-block puzzles are among the most famous games of all time. The original, Tetris, was created by Alexey Pajitnov at a computer research centre in Moscow in 1984. Its pieces, each made of four squares, have been falling ever since. Block Drop on this site is our version. These tips work for any falling-block game.

1. Keep the stack flat

Bumpy stacks with tall towers and deep holes leave few good places for the next piece. A flat, even surface fits almost every piece. When choosing where to drop, prefer the spot that keeps things level.

2. Avoid holes

A hole is an empty square with a block over it. You can't fill it until you clear the lines above it. One hole isn't a disaster, but several will slowly push your stack upwards. If a piece would leave a hole, look for another spot first.

3. Leave a well for the long piece

Clearing four lines at once scores far more than clearing them one at a time. To do it, build your stack across nine columns and leave one column empty (usually at the side). When the long straight piece comes, drop it in the gap and clear four rows together.

4. Look at Next

The Next box shows the coming pieces. Use it to plan: if a long piece is coming, don't fill your well with something else. If an awkward piece is coming, leave a spot that suits it.

5. Use Hold wisely

Hold lets you swap the current piece for later. It's perfect for saving a long piece for your well, or getting rid of a piece that doesn't fit anywhere right now.

6. Learn to rotate both ways

If you can only rotate one way, some placements take three rotations instead of one. Using both rotate buttons saves time, which matters a lot at higher levels.

7. Stay calm when it gets fast

At high speed, players often panic and start dropping pieces anywhere. Remember that a slightly imperfect placement is better than a rushed mistake. Use hard drop (Space) only when you're sure.

Why it's so popular

Falling-block puzzles are easy to understand, impossible to finish, and every game is different. Researchers have even studied them: some studies found that playing helped people get better at mentally rotating shapes, a skill used in maths and engineering.

Play

Block Drop has Hold, a Next preview and levels that speed up. Try building a well on the right side and see how many four-line clears you can get in one game.
