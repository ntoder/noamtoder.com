Word search tips: how to find words faster

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 5 min read

A word search hides a list of words in a grid of letters. Words can run across, down, diagonally and sometimes backwards. Some people find them relaxing, others find them frustrating. These tricks make them faster, and they're also good practice for reading and spelling.

1. Look for unusual letters

Letters like Q, Z, X, J and K don't appear often, so if a word contains one, look for that letter first. There will be only a few of them in the grid, and one of them is probably part of your word.

2. Use letter pairs, not single letters

Instead of looking for every "S" in the grid (there might be twenty), look for the first two letters together, like "ST" or "SH". Your eyes can spot a pair much faster, and there are far fewer of them.

3. Search one direction at a time

Scan every row from left to right looking for any word on your list. Then do every column, then the diagonals. This is often faster than hunting for one word at a time, because you're checking for all of them in a single pass.

4. Check backwards words last

Words written backwards are the hardest to see because we're used to reading one way. Once the forward words are found, go back and look for the end of each remaining word instead of the beginning.

5. Run your finger (or cursor) along

Following a row with your finger stops your eyes jumping around and helps you notice words you'd otherwise skip. On a screen, slowly moving the mouse along a row does the same thing.

6. Long words first

Long words take up more of the grid and are easier to spot because they have more letters in a recognisable order. Finding them early also removes lots of letters from consideration.

Why word searches are good for you

Word searches practise visual scanning (looking carefully across a page), spelling patterns, and focus. They're also a gentle way to learn new vocabulary: if a word on the list is new to you, look it up after you find it.

Make your own

If you're learning to code, a word search generator is a fun project: put words into a grid in random directions, check they don't clash, and fill the rest with random letters. It's a good exercise in loops and two-dimensional arrays.
