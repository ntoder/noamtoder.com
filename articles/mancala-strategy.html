Mancala tips: free turns, captures and counting stones

By Noam · 25 November 2026 · 6 min read

Mancala isn't one game but a whole family of "sowing" games played across Africa, the Middle East and Asia for many centuries. Players pick up all the stones from one pit and drop them one by one into the next pits. The version most people know today, sometimes called Kalah, has two rows of six pits and a big store at each end. That's the version on the Hub.

The rules in short

  • Pick any pit on your side and sow its stones one at a time to the right, around the board, including into your own store (but not your opponent's).
  • If your last stone lands in your store, you get a free turn.
  • If your last stone lands in an empty pit on your side, you capture it plus all the stones in the pit directly across, and put them in your store.
  • When one side is empty, the game ends. The other player keeps the stones left on their side, and the bigger store wins.

1. Look for free turns first

A pit with exactly the right number of stones to reach your store gives you an extra turn. The pit next to your store needs 1 stone, the one before it needs 2, and so on. Free turns are powerful because you can often chain them: take a free turn from the pit nearest your store first, because that one changes the fewest other pits.

2. Count before you move

Mancala rewards counting. Before every move, count where the last stone will land. It only takes a second and it's how you find captures, free turns, and the moves that would hand your opponent a capture.

3. Watch your empty pits

An empty pit on your side is a capture waiting to happen, if you can land a last stone there and the pit across from it is full. An empty pit on your opponent's side is a warning: check whether they can land in it and take your stones across the board.

4. Protect big piles

A pit full of stones is a target. If your opponent has a move that ends in the empty pit opposite it, either move the big pile away or fill the empty pit so the capture can't happen.

5. Keep stones on your side near the end

Because the leftover stones go to the player whose side they're on, hoarding stones in your pits (especially the ones far from your store) can win close games. Some players deliberately make small moves late in the game so their opponent runs out first.

Practise

Mancala on the Hub has a computer opponent and a two-player mode. A good exercise is to play one game where you count every move out loud before you make it. You'll be surprised how many free turns you were missing.
