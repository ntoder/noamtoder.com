Mine Finder: the patterns that let you solve without guessing

By Noam · 15 November 2026 · 7 min read

Mine Finder looks like a game of luck the first time you play. Then you learn to read the numbers, and it turns into a logic puzzle. Most of a board can be solved with certainty using a few simple rules and a couple of well-known patterns. Here's how.

What the numbers mean

A number tells you how many mines touch that square, counting all eight neighbours, including diagonals. A 1 means exactly one mine is somewhere around it. A blank square means zero, which is why blank areas open up automatically.

Rule 1: count the closed squares

If a number has exactly as many closed squares around it as its value, every one of those squares is a mine. A 1 with only one closed neighbour: that neighbour is a mine. A 3 with exactly three closed neighbours: all three are mines. Flag them.

Rule 2: count the flags

If a number already has as many flags around it as its value, every other closed square around it is safe. In our Mine Finder, you can click the number itself to open all its unflagged neighbours at once, which makes this very fast.

Those two rules solve a surprising amount of every board. Work around the edges of the open area, applying them to each number in turn.

Pattern: 1-2-1 along a wall

When you see the numbers 1, 2, 1 in a row along a straight edge of closed squares, the mines are under the two 1s, not under the 2. The square under the 2 is safe. It works because the 2 needs two mines, and the only way to give each 1 exactly one mine is to put them at the ends.

Pattern: 1-2-2-1 along a wall

For 1, 2, 2, 1 in a row along an edge, the mines are under the two 2s, and the squares under the 1s are safe. Once you've seen these two patterns a few times, you'll spot them instantly.

Pattern: a 1 in a corner

A 1 next to a corner of the open area, with two closed squares touching it, often has a neighbouring number that settles which one is the mine. Look at the numbers next to it: if one of them already has its mine accounted for, the shared square is safe and the mine is the other one.

Starting a board

In our game, your first click is always safe, and so are the squares around it. That means your first click opens a patch of the board with numbers to work from. Clicking near the middle tends to open a bigger area.

When you really have to guess

Occasionally a board reaches a point where no logic can decide, often a 50/50 between two squares at the end. When that happens, finish everything else first, then guess. If you have a choice of where to guess, a square far from any numbers is usually a better bet than a square next to a high number.

Getting faster

Speed comes from not hesitating. Learn the patterns well enough that you don't have to think about them, use number-clicking to open several squares at once, and use flag mode on a phone so a tap places a flag. Then try the bigger boards.
