8 mental math tricks that make you faster at sums

By Noam · 15 November 2026 · 6 min read

Being quick at mental maths isn't a special talent. Fast people mostly know a few shortcuts that turn hard sums into easy ones. Here are eight that are worth learning, with examples you can try in your head right now.

1. Adding 9: add 10, take away 1

47 + 9 is awkward. 47 + 10 is easy: 57. Then take away 1: 56. The same works for 19 (add 20, take away 1) and 99 (add 100, take away 1).

2. Make friendly numbers

When adding several numbers, look for pairs that make 10 or 100. In 7 + 8 + 3 + 2, pair 7 with 3 and 8 with 2: 10 + 10 = 20. In 65 + 35 + 18, add 65 and 35 first to make 100, then add 18.

3. Subtract by counting up

For 82 − 47, count up from 47 instead: 47 to 50 is 3, 50 to 80 is 30, 80 to 82 is 2. Add them: 3 + 30 + 2 = 35. This is how many people work out change in a shop, and it avoids borrowing completely.

4. Multiplying by 5: multiply by 10 and halve

5 is half of 10. So 5 × 36 is the same as 10 × 36 = 360, halved: 180. Much easier than the five times table for big numbers.

5. Multiplying by 4: double, then double again

4 × 23: double 23 is 46, double 46 is 92. Doubling twice is easier than multiplying by 4 directly. For 8, double three times.

6. Multiplying by 9: use your fingers or the 10 trick

9 × 7 is 10 × 7 minus 7: 70 − 7 = 63. And there's a pattern in the nine times table: the digits of every answer up to 9 × 10 add up to 9 (18, 27, 36, 45…), which is a quick way to check your answer.

7. Multiplying by 11 (for two-digit numbers)

For 11 × 35, add the two digits: 3 + 5 = 8, and put the result in the middle: 385. If the digits add up to 10 or more, carry the 1: 11 × 57 is 5, then 5 + 7 = 12, so 5(12)7 becomes 627.

8. Split hard multiplications

For 6 × 47, split 47 into 40 and 7. 6 × 40 = 240 and 6 × 7 = 42. Add them: 282. Splitting a number into tens and ones turns almost any multiplication into two easy ones.

The one thing that helps most

All these tricks get much easier when you know your times tables up to 10 × 10 by heart. If one table keeps slowing you down, focus on just that one for a few days.

Practise in short bursts

Math Blitz gives you 60 seconds to answer as many sums as you can. Every correct answer adds a second, but a wrong answer takes away three, so it rewards being accurate, not just fast. You can pick a single operation, like multiplication only, which is perfect for practising one trick at a time. Try playing one round a day and watch your score climb.
