How to design a good game level: 8 tips for young game makers

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 7 min read

Making a game is one thing. Making levels that are fun to play is another skill altogether, and professional game studios have people whose whole job is level design. If you're building your own game, or making levels in a level editor, these ideas will help your levels feel fair, clear and exciting.

1. Teach one idea at a time

Every new thing in your game (a jump pad, a moving enemy, a key that opens a door) should be introduced on its own, in a safe place, before you combine it with anything else. Many famous games teach without any text at all: the first time a new object appears, the level is shaped so the player has to use it, and failing isn't dangerous.

2. Then test it, then twist it

A classic pattern for designing with a new idea is: introduce it safely, test it with a bit of danger, twist it (combine it with something else or use it in an unexpected way), and finally finish with a harder challenge. This rhythm keeps levels feeling fresh.

3. Make it fair

Players accept losing when they understand why. They get annoyed when they're killed by something they couldn't see, or a jump that only works with pixel-perfect timing. Some rules that help:

  • Let the player see danger before it hurts them.
  • Give a little extra room on jumps and hit boxes. Being slightly generous makes games feel better.
  • Put checkpoints before hard sections, so a mistake doesn't send the player back too far.

4. Difficulty should go up and down

A level that gets harder and harder with no rest is exhausting. Good levels follow a wave: a hard bit, then an easier bit to breathe, then something harder. The overall difficulty goes up, but not in a straight line.

5. Guide with design, not arrows

Players look at bright things, moving things and open spaces. Use light, colour and shape to show the way. A coin or star placed along the right path quietly tells players where to go.

6. Reward curiosity

Hidden areas, secret paths and bonus items make players feel clever. They don't need to be big. A single extra star behind a waterfall is enough to make someone explore every corner of your next level.

7. Keep levels short

Especially for first games, short levels are better than long ones. A level that takes 30 to 90 seconds to finish once you know it is easy to retry and easy to test. You can always make more levels.

8. Watch someone play

This is the most important tip. Sit next to a friend or family member while they play your level, and don't help them. Watch where they get stuck, what they miss and where they smile. You'll learn more from five minutes of watching than from an hour of playing it yourself, because you already know the solution.
