How to learn the world's flags: tricks for telling similar flags apart

By Noam · 15 November 2026 · 6 min read

There are nearly 200 countries in the world, and many of their flags look almost the same. Learning them all sounds impossible, but flags follow patterns. Once you see the patterns, you're not memorising 200 random pictures. You're learning a few families and the small details that separate their members.

Learn in families

Many flags share designs because the countries share history or ideas.

  • Nordic crosses. Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland and Iceland all have a cross shifted toward the flagpole. Learn the colours: Denmark red and white, Sweden blue and yellow, Finland white and blue, Norway and Iceland red, white and blue in opposite arrangements.
  • Pan-African colours. Red, yellow and green appear on many African flags, including Ghana, Senegal, Mali, Cameroon and Ethiopia.
  • Pan-Arab colours. Red, white, black and green appear on flags like those of Jordan, the United Arab Emirates and Kuwait.
  • Three stripes. Lots of European flags are three bands of colour, either vertical like France and Italy, or horizontal like Germany and the Netherlands.

Spot the difference in look-alikes

Some pairs are famously confusing. Here's how to separate them.

  • Ireland and Ivory Coast both have green, white and orange. Ireland has green next to the flagpole; Ivory Coast has orange there.
  • Italy and Mexico both have green, white and red. Mexico has an eagle in the middle.
  • Indonesia and Monaco are both red over white and are nearly identical. Poland is the same two colours the other way up: white over red.
  • Romania and Chad are blue, yellow and red stripes with only a tiny difference in the shade of blue. In a quiz, look for other clues, such as the region you're playing.
  • Netherlands and Luxembourg are both red, white and blue horizontal stripes. Luxembourg's blue is lighter.

Use the symbols

A symbol often gives a country away instantly: the maple leaf of Canada, the cedar tree of Lebanon, the Star of David of Israel, the wheel of India, the sun of Argentina and Uruguay. When you meet a new flag, look for its symbol first.

Remember the unusual ones

A few flags break the rules, and that makes them easy to remember. Nepal's flag isn't a rectangle at all; it's two stacked triangles. Switzerland and Vatican City have square flags. Bhutan's flag has a dragon on it.

The best way to practise

Practise one region at a time. In Flag Quiz you can choose Europe, Asia, the Americas or Africa instead of the whole world. Play a region until you get ten out of ten, then move on.

Use both directions. "Name the flag" shows a flag and asks for the country. "Find the flag" shows a country name and asks for its flag. Switching between them is much better for memory than repeating one, because you have to recall the information instead of just recognising it.

Finally, pay attention to your mistakes. If you mix up the same two flags twice, stop and compare them side by side for a moment. That one look is worth ten more guesses.
