How to remember more: memory tricks for card-matching games

By Noam · 25 November 2026 · 6 min read

Memory card games, where you flip two cards at a time and try to find pairs, are often played by young children, but adults don't automatically beat them. That's because remembering lots of positions is hard for everyone. Short-term memory can only hold a handful of things at once. The good news is that a few tricks used by memory champions work on a small scale too.

1. Have a system for flipping

Flip cards in order, for example row by row from the top-left, instead of jumping around randomly. When cards are revealed in a pattern, it's much easier to remember where each one was, because you're remembering one path instead of many separate spots.

2. Say it out loud (or in your head)

Naming what you see, "cat, top corner; star, second row middle", uses your hearing memory as well as your seeing memory. Two kinds of memory holding the same fact are better than one.

3. Chunk the board

Instead of remembering 16 separate positions, split the board into four small corners of four cards each. "Top-left corner: cat, sun, car, fish" is one chunk, and your brain can hold several chunks much more easily than many single facts. This trick, called chunking, is the same reason phone numbers are split into groups.

4. Make it a story

Link cards next to each other into a silly picture: "The cat is driving the car to the sun." Weird, funny or exciting images stick far better than plain lists. That's why memory champions make their stories as strange as possible.

5. The memory palace

For longer lists, imagine walking through a place you know well, like your home. Put each thing you want to remember in a different spot along the way: a cat on the doormat, a car in the hallway, a sun in the kitchen. To remember the list, walk the route in your head. This method has been used since ancient Greece and is still the main technique in memory competitions.

6. Use your mistakes

A wrong guess still shows you two cards. Don't look away in frustration. Those are two new facts you now know for later turns.

7. Short, regular practice

Memory improves with practice like any other skill, and short sessions spread over several days work better than one long session. Sleep helps too: the brain sorts and stores what you learned during the day while you sleep.

Try it

Memory Match is the classic card-flipping game, and Memory Grid flashes a pattern of squares for you to repeat. Try the chunking trick in both and see if your score goes up.
