Pinball tips: how to trap the ball, aim your shots and score more

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

Most beginners play pinball by hitting both flippers the moment the ball comes near. Sometimes that works, but mostly it sends the ball flying in random directions, often straight down the middle. Good players do something different: they control the ball. Here's how.

1. Catch the ball (the cradle)

When the ball is coming down towards a flipper, hold that flipper up. The ball rolls down the flipper and stops in the corner between the flipper and the wall. This is called a cradle or a trap. Now the ball is still, and you can take your time to choose a shot. Trapping the ball is the single most useful pinball skill.

2. Aim with timing

Where the ball goes depends on where on the flipper it is when you flip. Flip early, when the ball is near the tip, and it goes across the table. Flip late, when the ball is close to the base of the flipper, and it goes more straight up on the same side. From a cradle, let the ball roll down slowly and flip at the moment it reaches the spot you want.

3. Pass the ball between flippers

Sometimes a shot is easier from the other flipper. From a cradle, a quick, gentle tap can bounce the ball across to the other flipper, where you can catch it again. This is called a post pass or a tap pass, and it lets you set up the shot you want instead of the one you happen to have.

4. Don't flip both flippers at once

Flipping both together makes a gap in the middle and doesn't let you aim. Use one flipper at a time, unless the ball is dropping right between them.

5. Learn the table

Every table has a few shots that are worth the most: ramps, targets that light up bonuses, or lanes that open features. Watch which shots raise your score fastest and aim for those once the ball is under control. Bumpers are fun, but the ball bounces around unpredictably near them.

6. Nudge carefully

On real machines, players push the cabinet to change the ball's path a little, called nudging. Too much and the machine "tilts", turning off the flippers. If a game has a nudge button, use it to save a ball heading for an outlane, and use it gently.

A strange bit of history

Early pinball machines had no flippers at all; players only launched the ball and hoped. Flippers appeared in 1947 and turned pinball into a game of skill. Even so, many cities treated pinball as gambling. New York City banned it from 1942 until 1976, when a player named Roger Sharpe helped overturn the ban by demonstrating to the city council that he could call a shot and hit it.
