Mini golf maths: how to use angles and bank shots

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 5 min read

Mini golf is all about walls. On a real course, and in the Mini Golf game on this site, you often can't hit the ball straight at the hole. Instead, you bounce it off a wall. The good news is that bounces follow a simple rule, and once you know it, you can plan shots instead of guessing.

The rule: angle in equals angle out

When a ball hits a flat wall, it bounces off at the same angle it came in, just on the other side. If it hits the wall at a shallow angle, it leaves at a shallow angle. If it hits head-on, it comes straight back. This is called the law of reflection, and it's the same rule that light follows when it bounces off a mirror.

The mirror trick

Here's how to aim a bank shot. Imagine the wall is a mirror, and picture where the hole would appear "inside" the mirror, the same distance behind the wall as the real hole is in front of it. Now aim straight at that imaginary hole. The ball will hit the wall at exactly the right spot and bounce towards the real hole. Pool and snooker players use this same trick.

Two bounces

For a shot off two walls, mirror the hole in the second wall, then mirror that point again in the first wall, and aim at the final point. It sounds complicated, but try it on paper once and it makes sense.

Power matters too

Real balls lose a little speed every time they hit a wall and as they roll, because of friction. That means:

  • Bank shots need more power than a straight shot of the same distance.
  • A ball that's going too fast can bounce out of the hole or overshoot. A ball that arrives slowly is more likely to drop in.
  • Aim to have the ball reach the hole at a gentle speed, not a crash.

Obstacles and slopes

Slopes curve the ball's path, and water or sand traps punish mistakes. When there's a hazard, it's often smarter to play two safe shots than one risky one. A "lay up" shot that leaves the ball in an easy spot is a real golf strategy.

Why this is useful outside golf

The law of reflection explains how mirrors work, why you can see yourself in a shop window, how periscopes let submarines see above water, and how light travels along optical fibres for the internet. A mini golf course is a fun way to see a real physics rule in action.

Play

Mini Golf on the Hub has nine holes with walls, water and sand. Try the mirror trick on any hole where you can't see a straight path, and see if you can get under par. For more on how games handle bounces, read how game physics works.
