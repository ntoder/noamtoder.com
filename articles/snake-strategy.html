Snake strategy: how to get a really long snake

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 5 min read

Snake is one of the simplest games ever made: eat the food, grow longer, don't crash into the walls or yourself. Early on it's easy. The game really starts when your snake is so long that it fills half the board. These strategies help you get there and survive.

1. Don't rush the food

Going straight for the food is fine while the snake is short. Once it's longer, the shortest path is often the most dangerous one, because it can leave your tail blocking the way out. Take a slightly longer route that keeps open space ahead of you.

2. Stay near the walls

Moving along the edges keeps your body out of the middle of the board, where it would split the space into small pockets. A snake that hugs the walls leaves one big open area in the centre, which is much safer.

3. Sweep in zig-zags

For a very long snake, a common strategy is to move in long back-and-forth lines across the board, like mowing a lawn. Your body then fills the board neatly in rows instead of in tangles, and there's always a path. It's slower, but it's how players reach huge lengths.

4. Never close a loop

The most common way to lose with a long snake is to curl around and leave your head inside a space that's smaller than your body. Before turning into a pocket, ask: "Is there enough room in there for me to get out again?" If not, don't go in.

5. Remember the tail moves

Your tail moves out of the way as you move forward. That means it's usually safe to follow your own tail closely. Some players use "chase your tail" as a safe holding pattern while they wait for food to appear somewhere easy.

6. Plan two turns ahead

At high speed you don't have time to think after each turn. Get used to deciding your next two turns early, especially near walls and corners.

Where Snake came from

The idea goes back to an arcade game called Blockade in 1976, where two players left trails behind them and tried to make the other crash. Versions for home computers followed. Snake became world-famous in 1997, when it was included on Nokia mobile phones, and for millions of people it was the first game they ever played on a phone.
