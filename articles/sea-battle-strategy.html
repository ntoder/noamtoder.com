Sea Battle strategy: how to find ships faster

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

In Sea Battle (the classic "Battleship" game), each player hides a fleet on a grid and takes turns guessing squares to shoot at. It feels like pure luck, but it isn't. Good shooting can find a fleet in far fewer shots than random guessing, and good hiding makes your fleet much harder to find.

Two modes: hunting and targeting

Every shot is either hunting (looking for a new ship) or targeting (finishing off a ship you've already hit). Treat them very differently.

Hunting: use a checkerboard

The smallest ship is 2 squares long, so it always covers one light and one dark square of a checkerboard pattern. That means you only need to shoot at half the squares (say, just the "dark" ones) to be sure of hitting every ship at least once. Shooting in a checkerboard pattern can cut your hunting shots roughly in half.

Once the small patrol boat is sunk, the smallest remaining ship is 3 long, and you can spread your shots out even more, for example every third square in each row, shifting the pattern on each row.

Hunt where ships fit

A ship can only be in a place big enough to hold it. Corners and edges have fewer ways for a ship to fit through them, and so do squares surrounded by misses. Squares in open areas, with lots of space in every direction, are the most likely to hide a ship. When you're not sure where to shoot next, pick the square with the most room around it.

Targeting: finish the ship

  • After a first hit, try the four squares around it (up, down, left, right).
  • After a second hit, you know which way the ship is pointing. Keep shooting along that line in both directions until it sinks.
  • If you get hits that don't line up, you've probably found two ships touching each other. Finish one, then come back for the other.

In the Hub's version a hit lets you fire again, so targeting well is even more valuable: one good hit can turn into a whole sunk ship in a single turn.

Hiding your own fleet

  • Don't make patterns. Ships all in a row, or all on one side, are easy to find once one is hit.
  • Mix directions. Use both horizontal and vertical ships.
  • Avoid obvious spots. Many players think the edges are safe, so experienced players check them. There is no perfectly safe spot, which is the point: be unpredictable.
  • Touching ships confuse people. Two ships side by side can make an opponent think they've found one big ship.

A bit of history

Guessing games like this were played with pencil and paper in the early 20th century, long before the plastic board game version became popular in the 1960s. Early home computers had versions too, because the game is simple to program but fun to play against a computer.

Try it

In Sea Battle on the Hub you can shuffle your fleet or place it yourself. Try one game shooting only on a checkerboard pattern and count how many shots it takes compared with your usual game.
