Brick-breaking games: the story of Breakout and how to clear every level

By Noam · 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

Brick-breaking games are simple: bounce a ball off a paddle to smash a wall of bricks without letting the ball fall past you. The idea is fifty years old and still everywhere, from arcades to phones. Here's where it came from, and how to play it well.

Breakout and two famous Steves

The game that started it all was Breakout, released by Atari in 1976. It grew out of Atari's earlier hit Pong: instead of two players hitting a ball back and forth, one player bounced it into a wall. The prototype hardware was designed by Steve Wozniak, working with Steve Jobs, who was an Atari employee at the time. The same year, the two of them founded Apple. Ideas from Breakout even influenced the design of the Apple II computer, which Wozniak wanted to be able to run games like it.

Arkanoid and power-ups

In 1986, a game called Arkanoid added power-ups that fall from broken bricks: a wider paddle, multiple balls, lasers. Most modern brick-breakers, including Brick Smash on this site, follow that idea.

Tip 1: The paddle controls the angle

In most brick-breaking games, the ball's bounce angle depends on where it hits the paddle. Hit it with the middle and it goes up steeply. Hit it near an edge and it flies off at a sharp angle towards that side. Once you know this, you can aim: move so the ball hits the part of the paddle that sends it where you want.

Tip 2: Break through to the top

The best feeling in the game is getting the ball above the bricks, where it bounces between the ceiling and the top of the wall, smashing bricks by itself. To do it, focus on breaking a tunnel through one side, usually near a wall where the ball naturally travels.

Tip 3: Watch the ball, not the bricks

It's tempting to look at the bricks you're about to break. But you lose by missing the ball. Keep your eyes on the ball as it comes down, and only glance up when it's travelling away from you.

Tip 4: Stay near the middle

Returning towards the centre between hits means you never have far to move. When the ball speeds up later in a level, this makes a big difference.

Tip 5: Be careful with power-ups

Chasing a falling power-up to the edge of the screen is a classic way to miss the ball. Only grab it if the ball is still high up.
