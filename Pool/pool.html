About Noam Pool

Eight-ball pool on a full table. Break the rack, claim solids or stripes, pot all seven of your balls and then sink the black 8-ball to win. Play against the computer on easy, medium or hard, or pass the mouse to a friend for a two-player game. A guide line shows where the white ball will hit and which way the object ball will go, and fouls give your opponent the ball in hand.

Tips

  • The first ball you hit must be one of yours. Hitting the wrong ball first is a foul.
  • Gentle shots are easier to control than hard ones.
  • A green line means you're aiming at your own ball. Red means it's a foul.
  • Don't pot the 8-ball until all your other balls are down, or you lose!
  • With the ball in hand, put the white ball where you have an easy shot.

How to get better at Noam Pool

Eight-ball is a game of planning. Before every shot, look for the easiest ball to pot, but also think about where the white ball will stop. A shot that pots a ball and leaves the white ball stuck behind the 8-ball can cost you the game, so softer, controlled shots usually beat big hits.

Use the guide line. The dotted line shows where the white ball travels, the circle shows where it touches the first ball, and the short coloured line shows the direction that ball will roll. If the short line points at a pocket, you're lined up. If it's red, you're about to hit the wrong ball and give away a foul.

When there's no easy pot, play safe: hit one of your balls gently so the white ball ends up somewhere awkward for your opponent. And remember the ball-in-hand rule: if the other player fouls, you can put the white ball anywhere, so pick a spot with a straight shot.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Play Easy. The computer misses a lot, and you can learn how the guide line works.
  • Getting good: Move up to Medium. Start thinking one shot ahead and play safe when there's no clean pot.
  • Expert: On Hard the computer rarely misses. Plan your whole run of balls before you start, and leave the 8-ball near a pocket.

Did you know?

Modern pool tables come from billiards, which started as an outdoor lawn game a bit like croquet. When it moved indoors onto tables, the green cloth was chosen to look like grass.

Questions players ask

What happens if I pot the white ball?

That's a scratch, which is a foul. Your opponent gets the ball in hand and can put the white ball anywhere on the table.

How are solids and stripes decided?

The table is open until someone pots a ball after the break. That player takes that group, and the other player gets the other group.

When can I pot the 8-ball?

Only after all seven of your balls are down. Pot it earlier, or scratch while potting it, and you lose.

Can I play with a friend?

Yes. Choose 2 players on the start screen and take turns on the same computer or phone.
