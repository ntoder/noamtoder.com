Nine Men's Morris

Take turns placing your 9 pieces on the dots. Three of your pieces in a straight line is a mill, and a mill lets you remove one of your rival's pieces!
When everyone has placed their pieces, slide them along the lines to make more mills. With only 3 pieces left you can fly anywhere. Leave your rival with 2 pieces, or no moves, to win.

Game Over

Try to set up a piece that can slide back and forth between two mills. You'll make a mill every turn!

About Nine Men's Morris

One of the oldest board games in the world. The board has 24 dots on three nested squares joined by lines. Each player has nine pieces. First you take turns placing them; then you take turns sliding them to a neighbouring empty dot. Whenever you line up three of your pieces along a line, you've made a mill and can remove an opponent's piece, though not one that's in a mill unless there's no other choice. A player down to three pieces may fly to any empty dot. Reduce your opponent to two pieces, or leave them stuck with no move, to win.

How to play

Tap a dotPlace a piece
Tap your piece, then a dotMove it

Tips

  • Pieces in a mill are safe from being removed (unless all are in mills).
  • Block your rival when they have two in a line.
  • Spread out during placement so you can move later.

How to get better at Nine Men's Morris

Nine Men's Morris has two halves. During placement, don't just rush to make a mill: your rival will block it. Instead, set up two possible mills at once, so they can only block one.

In the moving phase, mobility matters. A piece with no empty neighbour is stuck. Keep your pieces able to move, and try to box your rival's pieces in. If they can't move at all, you win.

The strongest pattern is a 'running mill': a piece that can slide out of a mill and back in, making a new mill every two turns. Each time, you remove one of your rival's pieces.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Make your first mill.
  • Getting good: Beat the computer.
  • Expert: Win with all nine pieces still on the board... almost!

Did you know?

A Nine Men's Morris board was found carved into the roof of an ancient Egyptian temple from around 1400 BC, and boards have been found on Viking ships too.

Questions players ask

What is a mill?

Three of your pieces in a straight line on the board's lines.

What does 'flying' mean?

When you're down to three pieces, you can move a piece to any empty dot, not just a neighbouring one.
