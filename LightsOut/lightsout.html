About Lights Out

A famous light-switch puzzle. Every tap flips a light and its four neighbours, so turning one light off can turn others on. The grid grows from 3 by 3 to 5 by 5 as you go, and every level tells you how many taps it can be solved in, so you can try for a perfect score.

How to play

Click or tap a lightFlip it and its neighbors

Tips

  • The order of taps doesn't matter, only which lights you tap.
  • Tapping a light twice cancels out.
  • Work row by row: fix each row by tapping the row below it.

How to get better at Lights Out

Two facts make Lights Out much easier. First, the order you tap in doesn't matter: tapping A then B gives exactly the same board as B then A. Second, tapping the same light twice does nothing. So a solution is really just a set of lights to tap once each.

A great method is called 'chasing the lights'. Go through the rows from top to bottom. For every light that's on in a row, tap the light directly below it. That switches it off without touching the rows above. When you reach the bottom, only the last row has lights left.

If lights are left in the bottom row, there's a pattern of taps in the top row that fixes it. Experiment, then chase the lights down again. With practice you'll recognise the patterns.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Levels 1 and 2 use a 3 by 3 grid. Get a feel for how the lights connect.
  • Getting good: From level 3 the grid is 4 by 4. Try chasing the lights row by row.
  • Expert: From level 6 it's 5 by 5. Aim for the target number of taps.

Did you know?

Lights Out was released as a handheld electronic toy in 1995. Mathematicians solve it with linear algebra, treating each light as a 0 or 1 and each tap as adding 1.

Questions players ask

Is every level solvable?

Yes, every level is made by tapping lights on a solved board, so it can always be undone.

How do I get more points?

Solve the level in the number of taps shown, or close to it.
