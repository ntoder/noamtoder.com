Paint Territory

You're the blue square. Steer with the arrow keys or swipe. Leave your land to draw a trail, then come back home to fill in everything inside the loop!

Game Over

Make small, quick loops when other players are near. Go for big loops only when the coast is clear.

About Paint Territory

A land-grab game for quick thinkers. You and four computer players each start with a small square of land. When you leave your land you leave a trail, and when you return, everything inside your loop becomes yours. But while you're out, your trail is your weak spot: if another player crosses it, you're out. Cut their trails to wipe them out and take back the map. After two minutes, see how much of the map you own.

How to play

Arrow keys, WASD or swipeSteer

Tips

  • Shorter trips are safer.
  • Hitting your own trail knocks you out too!
  • When you knock out a player, their land disappears for anyone to take.

How to get better at Paint Territory

Paint Territory is about risk. The longer you stay outside your land, the bigger your loop, but also the longer your trail is exposed. Watch where the other squares are, and turn home as soon as one comes near.

A good pattern is a thin rectangle: go out a few squares, turn, run along the edge of your land, then turn back in. You gain a strip of land quickly without straying far from home.

Attacking is powerful. If a computer player is out on a long trail, cut across it and they're gone, along with all their land. Just make sure your own trail is short when you do it.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Own 10% of the map.
  • Getting good: Knock out 3 players.
  • Expert: Own 40% of the map.

Did you know?

Mathematicians call the area-filling trick in this game a flood fill. It's the same idea a paint program uses when you click with the paint bucket.

Questions players ask

How do I claim land?

Leave your land, draw a loop, and come back. Everything inside the loop becomes yours.

What knocks me out?

Another player touching your trail, hitting your own trail, or hitting the edge of the map.
