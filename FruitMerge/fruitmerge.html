About Fruit Merge

A juicy physics puzzle. Drop fruit into the box one at a time. When two of the same fruit bump into each other, they squish together into the next, bigger fruit: cherries become strawberries, strawberries become grapes, and so on all the way up to a giant watermelon. Fruit rolls, stacks and bounces with real physics, and one merge can set off a chain reaction. The game ends when the fruit piles up past the line at the top.

Tips

  • Keep your biggest fruit in a corner, with smaller ones lined up next to it in order.
  • Look at the next fruit (top right) before you drop, so you can plan two moves ahead.
  • Small fruit can fill the gaps between big ones.
  • Chain merges score bonus points.
  • Don't drop fruit on top of a much bigger one in the middle of the box.

How to get better at Fruit Merge

The secret to big scores in Fruit Merge is order. Pick a corner of the box and keep your biggest fruit there. Then try to line up the next sizes beside it, getting smaller as you go, like steps. When a new fruit merges at the small end, it grows into the size next to it and can trigger a chain that rolls all the way along the line.

Always check the 'next' fruit in the top right corner before you drop. If the next fruit is a match for something on the board, you can save space for it. If it isn't, drop the current fruit somewhere it won't block your chain.

Small fruit is the biggest danger, because cherries and strawberries get trapped under big fruit where they can't reach their partners. Drop them close to another of the same kind, or into gaps on the small side of your chain. If the pile gets close to the dashed line, focus only on merges that free up space.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Just try to make an apple. Drop matching fruit next to each other.
  • Getting good: Build a corner chain and reach the pineapple.
  • Expert: Plan with the next fruit, set off chain reactions, and go for the watermelon.

Did you know?

Watermelons are about 92% water. Their wild ancestors grew in Africa and were bitter. The sweet red watermelon we eat today came from thousands of years of careful farming.

Questions players ask

When does the game end?

When fruit stays above the dashed line near the top for about two seconds.

What's the biggest fruit?

The watermelon. If two watermelons touch, they both disappear for a huge bonus.

Why does the fruit roll away?

The fruit uses real physics, so round fruit rolls off slopes and bounces off the others.

How do I score more?

Bigger fruit is worth more points, and merges that happen one after another give a combo bonus.
