About Jigsaw

A relaxing jigsaw puzzle. The game paints a picture (a sunset over a lake, an underwater reef, a city at night, or a flower meadow), then cuts it into real jigsaw-shaped pieces with tabs and slots. Drag each piece into the frame. A faint copy of the picture shows where things belong, and pieces snap into place when they're close. Pick 9, 16, 25 or 36 pieces.

Tips

  • Flat-sided pieces go on the edges.
  • Corner pieces have two flat sides.
  • Group pieces by colour first.

How to get better at Jigsaw

Jigsaw puzzlers almost always start with the edges. Pieces with one flat side belong on the border, and pieces with two flat sides are the corners. Building the frame first gives you a shape to fill in.

Next, sort by colour. Sky pieces are usually lighter, ground or water pieces darker. The faint picture in the frame is a guide, so match the colours of a piece to the area where it looks the same.

Take your time. There's a timer if you want a challenge, but jigsaws are also great for relaxing. Try 9 pieces for a quick puzzle or 36 for a long one.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Finish a 9-piece puzzle.
  • Getting good: Finish a 25-piece puzzle.
  • Expert: Finish a 36-piece puzzle.

Did you know?

The first jigsaw puzzles were made around 1760 by a London mapmaker. He glued maps onto wood and cut them along country borders to help children learn geography.

Questions players ask

How do pieces snap?

Drop a piece near its correct spot and it clicks into place.

Is the picture always the same?

No, the game paints a new version each time and rotates through four scenes.
