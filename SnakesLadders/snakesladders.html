About Snakes & Ladders

The classic family board game. Up to four players race their counters across a 100-square board. Roll the die and move that many squares. If you stop at the bottom of a ladder you climb to the top, but if you stop on a snake's head you slide down to its tail. Rolling a 6 gives another turn, and you need the exact number to finish on square 100. Play against the computer or pass the device around with friends.

  • The ladder at 4 goes all the way to 25.
  • The snake on 99 is the worst one, so be careful near the end.
  • A 6 always gives an extra turn.

How to get better at Snakes & Ladders

Snakes & Ladders is a game of pure luck, so there's no wrong move. Every turn you roll and move. That makes it great for playing with younger kids or anyone who wants a relaxed game.

Even though you can't choose, it's fun to know the board. Big ladders are on 4, 13 and 50, and the nastiest snakes are on 99, 89 and 76. The last row is full of danger.

You need the exact number to land on 100. If you're on 97, only a 3 will do. Any bigger number and your turn is skipped, which can lead to some exciting finishes.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Win a game against the computer.
  • Getting good: Win a 4-player game.
  • Expert: Climb three ladders in one game.

Did you know?

Snakes & Ladders comes from an old game played in India called Moksha Patam. The ladders stood for good deeds and the snakes for bad ones.

Questions players ask

How many people can play?

Two to four, in any mix of people and computer players.

What happens if I roll too high at the end?

You don't move, and the turn passes on.
