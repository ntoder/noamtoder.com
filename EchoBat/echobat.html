Echo Bat

You're a bat, and the cave is completely dark! Press Space (or tap) to flap up. Gravity pulls you down.
Press S (or the 🔊 button, or right-click) to send a sonar ping: a ring of sound that lights up the walls for a moment. Sonar recharges slowly, and glowing moths give you extra. Don't touch the walls or the crystal spikes!

About Echo Bat

A flying game where you can't see. Real bats find their way in the dark using echolocation: they make high-pitched sounds and listen to the echoes. In Echo Bat, each sonar ping sends out a glowing ring that briefly reveals the cave walls and the sharp crystals ahead, then everything fades back to black. Flap to stay in the air, ping to see, and catch moths to recharge your sonar. The cave gets narrower and faster the deeper you go.

Tips

  • You can only see the walls for a moment after each ping.
  • You have up to 3 pings stored. They recharge over time.
  • Moths give extra sonar and bonus distance.

How to get better at Echo Bat

Echo Bat is a memory game as much as a flying game. A ping lights up the cave ahead for a moment. Look at the shape of the tunnel, remember where it goes up or down, then fly by memory while it fades.

Sonar is limited: three pings that recharge slowly. Don't ping constantly. Ping when you're about to reach new territory, especially when the cave bends.

Watch for crystal spikes. They stick out from the floor or ceiling and only show up in sonar. Moths glow on their own, so you can always see them. Catch them to refill your sonar.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Fly 200 metres.
  • Getting good: Catch 5 moths.
  • Expert: Fly 1,000 metres.

Did you know?

Bats can 'see' with sound. Some can detect a wire thinner than a human hair in complete darkness using echolocation.

Questions players ask

How do I use sonar?

Press S, ⬇, right-click, or the 🔊 button. You can store up to three pings.

Why is it so dark?

You're a bat in a cave! Only sonar pings and moths make light.
