About Hill Rider

A bumpy driving game with real physics. Your little car has springy suspension, so it bounces over bumps, flies off hills and tips over if you're not careful. Hold the gas to speed up and the brake to slow down, and use them in the air to tilt the car so you land on your wheels. Keep an eye on the fuel gauge and grab fuel cans along the way. Coins pay for upgrades to your engine, suspension, tires and fuel tank, and the scenery changes from meadows to desert to snow the further you drive.

How to play

➡ or DGas (tilts the car back in the air)

PhoneHold the GAS and BRAKE pedals

Tips

  • Let go of the gas going down steep hills so you don't flip forward.
  • In the air, tap gas or brake to tilt the car and land on both wheels.
  • Do a full flip in the air and land it for bonus coins.
  • Fuel cans come every so often, so don't waste time going backward.
  • Upgrade the engine first, then the fuel tank so you can drive further.

How to get better at Hill Rider

Hill Rider is about balance more than speed. The car's weight shifts as you drive: pressing gas pushes the nose up, and braking pushes it down. On a steep climb, ease off the gas if the front wheels start to lift, or you'll flip over backward. On the way down, let go of the gas and use a little brake so you don't nose-dive.

The air is where you win or lose. When the car leaves a hill, use gas and brake to tilt it so both wheels touch down together. A good landing keeps your speed for the next hill, while landing on your nose slows you right down, and landing on your head ends the run. Once you're confident, try a full flip in the air: land it cleanly and you earn bonus coins.

Fuel is the other limit. The gauge drains all the time and faster when you hold the gas, so keep moving forward and grab every ⛽ can you see. Spend coins in the garage between runs: a stronger engine climbs hills more easily, better suspension soaks up big landings, grippy tires stop you sliding back, and a bigger fuel tank lets you reach the desert and the snow.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Reach 500 m in the green meadows. Practise easing off the gas on hills.
  • Getting good: Get to the desert (800 m) and start using the air controls to land on your wheels.
  • Expert: Upgrade your fuel tank and engine and push into the snow beyond 1800 m.

Did you know?

Real cars use springs and shock absorbers in their suspension for the same reason this one does: the springs soak up bumps, and the shocks stop the car from bouncing up and down afterwards.

Questions players ask

Why did the game end?

Either the driver's head touched the ground, or you ran out of fuel and stopped.

How do I earn more coins?

Collect coins on the road, stay in the air for a long time, and land flips.

What should I upgrade first?

The engine helps on steep hills, and the fuel tank lets every run go further. Both are good first picks.

Does the road change?

Yes, the hills get bigger as you go, and the scenery changes from meadow to desert to snow.
