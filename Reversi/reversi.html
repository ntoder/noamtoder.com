Reversi

Place a black disc so it traps white discs in a straight line between two of yours. All the trapped discs flip to black!
Whoever has the most discs when the board is full wins.

About Reversi

The classic strategy board game, also known as Othello. You play black against the computer. Each move must trap one or more white discs in a straight line between your new disc and another black disc, and every trapped disc flips to your color. The lead can swing wildly right up to the last move, so think about corners and edges, not just about flipping as many as possible.

Tips

  • Grab the corners: they can never be flipped.
  • Don't play next to an empty corner, or the computer may take it.
  • Flipping fewer discs early often gives you more moves later.

How to get better at Reversi

Beginners try to flip as many discs as possible on every move, but in Reversi that usually backfires. A board full of your color gives your opponent lots of discs to trap. Strong players keep their disc count low in the middle of the game and focus on having more possible moves than their opponent.

The corners are the most important squares, because a disc in a corner can never be flipped. The squares diagonally next to a corner are the most dangerous ones: playing there often hands the corner to the other side. Edges are good too, but only once they're safe.

Near the end, count carefully. Because discs flip in big groups, a player who is far behind can win in the last few moves. Save a safe move for last when you can.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Play Easy and learn which moves are legal (the dots show them).
  • Getting good: On Medium, start grabbing corners and avoiding the squares next to them.
  • Expert: Hard looks a few moves ahead. Keep your options open and think about mobility.

Did you know?

The modern version of the game was published in Japan in 1971 under the name Othello, but a very similar game called Reversi was already popular in England in the 1880s.

Questions players ask

What if I can't move?

Your turn is skipped and the computer plays again. If neither side can move, the game ends.

Who goes first?

You do, with the black discs.
