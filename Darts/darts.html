About Darts

Darts, the classic pub and tournament game, in your browser. You start on 301 and every dart takes away what it hits. The thin outer ring doubles a number and the thin inner ring triples it. To win you must hit exactly zero, and the last dart must be a double or the bullseye. Your aim wobbles, so time your throw for when the crosshair drifts over your target. Try to finish in as few darts as you can.

Tips

  • Treble 20 is the biggest score on the board: 60.
  • Leave yourself an even number at the end so there's a double to finish on.
  • Watch the wobble and throw when the crosshair swings over your target.

How to get better at Darts

The fastest way to bring 301 down is the 20 at the top, especially the treble ring, which makes it worth 60. Three treble 20s is 180, the best possible turn. If you keep missing to the sides, try the treble 19 at the bottom: the numbers around it (7 and 3) are less painful misses than the 1 and 5 next to the 20.

Your crosshair wobbles in a pattern. Instead of chasing it, keep the mouse still on your target and watch it swing. Throw at the moment it passes through the spot you want.

The finish is the tricky part. You must land on exactly zero with a double. At 40 you need double 20, at 32 double 16 and so on. Try to leave an even number, ideally one that can be halved several times (32, 16, 8), so a near miss still leaves you a double.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Finish a game.
  • Getting good: Finish 301 in under 25 darts.
  • Expert: Score 100 or more in one turn.

Did you know?

The highest possible three-dart score is 180, from three treble 20s. Players and crowds shout it out in a famous long call: one hundred and eighty!

Questions players ask

What's a bust?

If your score goes below zero, to exactly 1, or to zero without a double, the whole turn is cancelled.

Why do I have to finish on a double?

That's the real rule in tournament darts. The bullseye (50) counts as a double too.
