About Color Lines

A classic puzzle from the 1990s. A 9×9 board slowly fills with coloured balls. Each turn you move one ball to any empty square it can reach by an open path. Make a line of five or more of the same colour, in any direction, to clear it and score points. Moves that don't clear a line bring three new balls onto the board, and the colours of the next three are shown at the top. How long can you keep the board from filling up?

Tips

  • Longer lines score much more than five.
  • Diagonals count too!
  • Use the preview to see which colours are coming.

How to get better at Color Lines

Color Lines is a game of planning ahead. Every move that doesn't clear a line adds three more balls, so try to build several lines at once. When you have four in a row in two places, every new ball of that colour helps.

Space is the real enemy. A ball surrounded by others can't move, and a board full of small separate gaps leaves no paths. Try to keep wide open rows and columns so balls can travel anywhere.

Clearing a line gives you a free move (no new balls appear). Lines longer than five score bonus points, so if it's safe, wait and make six or seven.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Score 100 points.
  • Getting good: Clear a line of 6.
  • Expert: Score 800 points.

Did you know?

Color Lines was made in Russia in 1992 and became one of the most popular computer games in Eastern Europe.

Questions players ask

Why can't I move a ball there?

There's no open path. Balls can't jump over other balls.

Do I get new balls after clearing a line?

No. Clearing a line is a free turn.
