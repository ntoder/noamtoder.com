Orbit Builder

Press anywhere in space, drag in the direction you want to throw the moon, and let go. The planet's gravity pulls it into a curve. The dotted line shows its path!

About Orbit Builder

A space physics toy with a goal. A planet sits in the middle of the screen with real gravity. Throw moons into space and watch them curve: too slow and they spiral into the planet, too fast and they escape into deep space, but just right and they circle in an orbit. Each level asks you to keep more moons orbiting at the same time, without any of them colliding. The predicted path line helps you aim.

Tips

  • Throw at right angles to the line to the planet.
  • Moons closer to the planet must move faster.
  • Give each moon its own lane at a different distance.

How to get better at Orbit Builder

Orbit Builder uses the same idea that keeps the real Moon going around Earth. A moon is always falling toward the planet, but if it's moving sideways fast enough, it keeps missing. To get an orbit, throw the moon sideways, at right angles to the line pointing to the planet.

Speed depends on distance. Close to the planet, moons need to go fast; far away, slowly. The dotted line shows the predicted path, so adjust your drag until the dots make a loop that doesn't touch the planet.

With several moons, collisions are the danger. Put each moon at a different distance from the planet, like lanes on a running track, and throw them all in the same direction around the planet.

From beginner to expert

  • Beginner: Get 3 moons orbiting.
  • Getting good: Reach level 3.
  • Expert: Keep 6 moons orbiting at once.

Did you know?

The International Space Station orbits Earth at about 28,000 km/h. It circles the whole planet roughly every 90 minutes.

Questions players ask

Why did my moon crash?

It was too slow, or aimed too much toward the planet.

Why did my moon fly away?

It was too fast. Escaping a planet's gravity needs extra speed.
